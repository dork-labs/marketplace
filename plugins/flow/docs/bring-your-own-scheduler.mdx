# Bring your own scheduler

`/flow` ships one schedulable tick: the `flow-drain` skill at
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/flow-drain/SKILL.md`. Each fire claims the top-ranked ready item,
carries it to your review gate, and stops. One tick, one item, a fresh session each time. Something
outside the tick has to provide the repetition.

## On DorkOS: nothing to wire

DorkOS finds the tick by itself. Any skill whose frontmatter carries a `schedule:` block is a
scheduled task, and DorkOS reads that from your skills roots — so installing `/flow` at project scope
is the whole setup step. There is no file to copy and no scheduler to stand up.

<Callout type="info">
  **Installing never starts anything.** A schedule DorkOS discovers arrives switched off and waits on
  the **Schedules** page for you to approve it. That holds no matter what the file says: `flow-drain`
  ships `schedule.enabled: false`, and even a `true` there would still park for your approval. See
  [Turning on the automatic loop](/docs/guides/flow/turning-on-autonomy) for the full picture, and
  [The dials](/docs/guides/flow/the-dials) for the cadence.
</Callout>

## On any other harness: wire a scheduler

Claude Code has no scheduler of its own (plugin "monitors" are session-scoped event streams, not
crons), so outside DorkOS the tick cannot fire itself. Point any external scheduler at it — an OS
`cron` line, a scheduled CI job, or a small poller — and run one `/flow continue` tick per fire. You
own that scheduler, so you also own its cadence and its uptime; running with your laptop closed needs
a scheduler that lives somewhere else, like CI.

With nobody at a terminal, a tick that has to stop and ask parks its question on the tracker and
nudges through the `comment-and-nudge` channel. Your reply is picked up by the next tick's inbox
poll, and the run resumes where it left off.
