# Bring your own scheduler

`/flow` ships one schedulable tick: the `flow-drain` skill at
`${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/flow-drain/SKILL.md`. Each fire claims the top-ranked ready item,
carries it to your review gate, and stops. One tick, one item, a fresh session each time. Something
outside the tick has to provide the repetition.

## On DorkOS: install at project scope, then approve

Any skill whose frontmatter carries a `schedule:` block is a scheduled task. A DorkOS release that
has schedule discovery reads that straight out of the skills roots it watches, so **installing
`/flow` at project scope is the whole setup step** — no file to copy, no scheduler to stand up. The
tick then appears on your **Schedules** page waiting to be approved.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Check your DorkOS version before relying on this.** Schedule discovery is rolling out; on a build
  without it, the tick in this plugin is an ordinary skill and nothing will fire it. Until your DorkOS
  has it, use the OS-cron / CI path below — it works on any version, and on no DorkOS at all.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
  **Installing never starts anything.** A discovered schedule arrives switched off and waits for you
  to approve it. That holds no matter what the file says: `flow-drain` ships `schedule.enabled:
  false`, and even a `true` there would still park for your approval. Approving switches it on, and
  that switch on the Schedules page is the one that counts from then on. See [Turning on the automatic
  loop](/docs/guides/flow/turning-on-autonomy) for the full picture. DorkOS fires the tick at
  flow's default cadence, the top of every hour; for another cadence, use a scheduler of your own
  (below) and leave `flow-drain` switched off on DorkOS. [The
  dials](/docs/guides/flow/the-dials#cadence-set-it-where-the-tick-is-scheduled) has the details.
</Callout>

## On any other harness: wire a scheduler

Claude Code has no scheduler of its own (plugin "monitors" are session-scoped event streams, not
crons), so the tick cannot fire itself. This is also the path to use on a DorkOS build that does not
have schedule discovery yet. Point any external scheduler at it — an OS
`cron` line, a scheduled CI job, or a small poller — and run one `/flow continue` tick per fire. You
own that scheduler, so you also own its cadence and its uptime; running with your laptop closed needs
a scheduler that lives somewhere else, like CI. Its own entry is the cadence: it never reads the
timing in flow's shipped `flow-drain` file, and a flow update never changes it.

`/flow:pause` works with any scheduler: it writes a pause flag into your project
(`.agents/flow/paused.json`) and every tick checks it first and stops, so your scheduler keeps
firing but nothing is claimed until `/flow:resume`. The flag belongs to the machine that wrote it; a
scheduler on another machine, such as CI, is paused by disabling that job.

With nobody at a terminal, a tick that has to stop and ask parks its question on the tracker and
nudges through the `comment-and-nudge` channel. Your reply is picked up by the next tick's inbox
poll, and the run resumes where it left off.
