# Turning on the automatic loop

<Callout type="info">
  **"Automatic" means the loop carries work to a check you chose, on its own.**
  The review gate (after VERIFY) and the calibration floor are *always checked*,
  in every setup on this page. The loop claims a ready item, drives it to a PR
  with evidence, parks at the review gate, and waits for its check. Unless you
  let the reviewer agent ship work it found clean (the project's "How much it
  does on its own" setting in DorkOS, see [The dials](/docs/guides/flow/the-dials)),
  you approve and merge. Nothing reaches `main` that nobody checked.
</Callout>

There are three ways to run `/flow` without hand-driving each stage. They differ on one axis:
**how the loop is scheduled.** What they share is the contract above: each carries one item along
the spine (`TRIAGE → … → VERIFY → REVIEW`) to its review gate, then stops.

## What "automatic" actually means

Before you pick a setup, know what the loop does and does not do. These behaviors are identical
whichever way you schedule it:

- **It runs one item at a time, to the gate.** Concurrency is `sequential`, WIP-capped (2 global,
  1 per project). The loop claims the top-ranked eligible item, carries it to REVIEW, parks, then
  claims the next. It is not a swarm.
- **It is fueled by `agent/ready`.** Dispatch only ever claims an item carrying the `agent/ready`
  label. That label is now _produced_ at two stage boundaries: TRIAGE applies it on accept (both
  the simple `task` route and the complex IDEATE route), and DECOMPOSE applies it to the
  execute-ready tasks it emits. No ready work means nothing to dispatch.
- **It never stops silently.** When the ready queue empties with shapeable work still behind the
  gate (backlog or triage items that lack `agent/ready`), the loop reports
  `0 ready, N shapeable: run a triage pass?` rather than reporting a bare "drain complete." That
  starvation signal comes from `classifyDispatchOutcome`, which separates "genuinely done" from
  "starved, feed me."
- **It never dead-ends on a question.** When a stage hits the calibration floor or a genuine
  uncertainty, it posts a readable question comment, applies `agent/needs-input`, and (in
  shared-account mode) pings you out of band. Your reply is detected on the next inbox poll and the
  run resumes where it left off, with no manual re-trigger.
- **Each tick runs the reconcilers in priority order.** A tick is not just "dispatch": it walks
  `recovery → inbox/resume → dispatch`, so a crashed item is recovered and a parked answer is
  picked up _before_ new work is claimed.

<Callout type="warn">
  The loop respects the **calibration floor** unconditionally. An action that is
  irreversible or destructive, outward-facing, spends money or touches secrets,
  or materially changes scope is checked *even at full confidence*: by you, or,
  at Just do it, by the reviewer agent. Never by nobody, and anything about
  secrets or spending always waits for you. The floor is non-trimmable: a config
  that tries to empty it fails to load. Autonomy can never be widened past the
  floor by editing a file.
</Callout>

## The three setups

| Setup                           | Needs the DorkOS server? | When it runs                                   | Driven by              | Shipped in v1?               |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **A — `/flow auto`**            | No                       | While your terminal session is open (attended) | You, from the terminal | Yes                          |
| **B — the Pulse seat**          | Yes                      | On a schedule, in the background               | The tracker (poll)     | Yes, off by default          |
| **C — the `claude -p` watcher** | No                       | On a schedule you operate                      | The tracker (poll)     | No — you build it; not in v1 |

## A — `/flow auto` (the attended terminal drain)

`/flow auto` drains the ready queue **sequentially from your terminal**. It is server-free: it talks
to the tracker through your tracker adapter (the one flow ships, or the one `/flow:init` generated
into your project) and runs each stage in your current session. This is the
fastest way to put the loop to work and the easiest to watch.

### Setup

<Steps>
  <Step>
    **Be in the repo, with the tracker reachable.** Your adapter reaches the tracker over the
    transport you chose at `/flow:init` — an in-session MCP server, or an account-pinned CLI. No
    server, no install.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Run `/flow auto`.** It claims the top-ranked `agent/ready` item, carries it to its review gate,
    parks, then claims the next-ranked item, until the queue is empty or starved. For a single tick
    instead of a full drain, run bare `/flow` and choose **Continue the queue**.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Watch it work.** Each item runs to a PR plus evidence and stops at REVIEW. If it starves, it
    asks whether to run a triage pass. If it parks on a question, answer in the terminal and it
    continues.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Close the loop yourself.** When you approve and merge a PR, run `/flow:done <issue>` to close
    the item, file follow-ups, and tear down the worktree.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Pros**

- Server-free: zero infrastructure, runs anywhere you have the repo and the adapter.
- Live and legible: you see every claim, every stage, every parked question as it happens.
- Single audit surface: all tracker I/O still flows through the one adapter.

**Cons**

- Attended only: it runs while your terminal session is open, not while you sleep. Close the
  session and the drain stops.
- Outside DorkOS you merge and run `/flow:done` by hand.

## B — the Pulse seat (the scheduled background loop)

The Pulse seat moves the same drain off your terminal and onto the DorkOS server. It is a
**file-based schedule** at `skills/flow-drain/` (in the plugin): DorkOS Pulse already provides a contextless
code-loop (a croner) that dispatches a fresh, isolated, runtime-agnostic agent session per run, so
there is no scheduler to build. **One tick equals one issue:** each croner fire is a fresh
run-session (`sessionId = run.id`) that claims and works exactly one ready item to its review gate,
then ends. That preserves a fresh session per issue and a durable, resumable checkpoint.

This is real today, but it ships **disabled by default** and depends on a running server.

### Setup

<Steps>
  <Step>
    **Start the DorkOS server.** Pulse hosts the watcher and the croner that
    fire the schedule. No server means no background ticks.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Register the project's DorkOS agent.** DorkOS watches that project's
    skills root, which is where it finds the tick; the project agent is what the
    tick operates as.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Approve the flow-drain tick on the Schedules page.** On a DorkOS release
    with schedule discovery, a schedule found in a file is never armed for you:
    `skills/flow-drain/SKILL.md` ships `enabled: false`, and the tick waits on
    **Schedules** until you approve it. Approving switches it on. That switch is
    yours to flip and it outlasts updates. Do not edit the file itself: it is
    the package's, and an update replaces it. On a build without discovery,
    wire an external scheduler instead ([Bring your own
    scheduler](/docs/guides/flow/bring-your-own-scheduler)).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Cadence and limits.** DorkOS fires the tick at flow's default, the top
    of every hour. To run it at another cadence, make a schedule of your own
    that runs `/flow continue`; see [where the cadence is
    set](/docs/guides/flow/the-dials#cadence-set-it-where-the-tick-is-scheduled).
    Cap the work in flight with `autonomy.wipCap`; see [The
    dials](/docs/guides/flow/the-dials).
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Watch and steer.** `/flow:status` shows every in-flight item, every parked
    question, and the rationale behind each decision. `/flow pause` halts every
    mode from one place; `/flow resume` lifts it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Pros**

- Unattended progress: ticks fire on a schedule with your terminal closed.
- Tracker-driven: the loop reacts to what is `agent/ready` in the tracker, polled on an interval.
- Fresh, isolated session per item, with crash and stall recovery from the durable `FlowRun`
  record.

**Cons**

- Needs a running DorkOS server: the seat cannot fire without it.
- Still stops at the review gate until someone checks it: you, or the reviewer agent if you let it
  ([below](#how-much-it-does-on-its-own)).
- Polls, not pushed: it reacts on the poll interval, not instantly (the webhook transport is
  deferred).

<Callout type="info">
  **What the Pulse seat is, precisely.** A *per-tick* scheduled drain: each fire is a fresh session
  that runs `recovery → inbox/resume → dispatch` and carries one item to its gate. The always-on
  runner that executes the reconciler registry as one process is the P5 server build.
</Callout>

## C — the `claude -p` watcher (portable, you build it)

The watcher seat is for repositories that are **not** DorkOS projects: an external poller you
operate (a cron entry, a CI loop, a small daemon) that fetches ready work through the same
`PollingTransport` seam and fires one headless `claude -p` tick per ready item. It needs no DorkOS
infrastructure, and nothing about the engine changes.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Not shipped in v1.** The watcher seat is designed but not built: `autonomy.seat` accepts only
  `pulse` today, and there is no `/flow:status` pane or recovery around it yet.
</Callout>

## The dials that govern any setup

These config dials shape the loop regardless of how you schedule it. They live in
`.agents/flow/config.json`. This is the short list; the full reference, with "when to change it"
guidance, is in [The dials](/docs/guides/flow/the-dials).

| Dial                                   | What it controls                                                                                           | Default                             |
| -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| `autonomy.default`                     | The default mode when you run `/flow` with no mode.                                                        | `"auto"`                            |
| `autonomy.concurrency`                 | How many items run at once. Fixed in v1; atomic concurrency is the server build.                           | `"sequential"`                      |
| `autonomy.wipCap`                      | Work-in-progress ceiling, global and per project.                                                          | `{ global: 2, perProject: 1 }`      |
| `autonomy.seat`                        | Which autonomous seat runs the loop. Only `pulse` validates in v1.                                         | `"pulse"`                           |
| `loops.<id>`                           | Per-reconciler `enabled` / `priority` / `intervalMs` (recovery, inbox, review, dispatch, triage, hygiene). | see config                          |
| `ingestion.producer`                   | The inbound transport. `poll` today; `webhook` is the deferred drop-in.                                    | `"poll"`                            |
| `ingestion.pollIntervalMs`             | How often the loop polls the tracker for inbound events.                                                   | `60000`                             |
| `gates.planApproval`                   | Whether DECOMPOSE pauses for a pre-code plan checkpoint.                                                   | `false`                             |
| `involvement.calibration.alwaysAsk`    | The non-trimmable floor: action classes that are always checked.                                           | four classes (min 1)                |
| `involvement.nudge`                    | The out-of-band attention channel for parked questions in shared mode.                                     | `{ relay: false, telegram: false }` |
| `dispatch.rank` / `dispatch.sizeOrder` | How the next item is chosen when several are ready.                                                        | unblockers-first, small-first       |

<Callout type="info">
  Adding or removing a control loop is a config edit, not a code change: register a reconciler to add
  one, set `loops.<id>.enabled: false` to remove one. The `loops` map is the extension seam.
</Callout>

## Which should I use?

| If you want to…                                                            | Use…                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Put the loop to work right now and watch it, with no setup                 | **A — `/flow auto`**                         |
| Run a single tick (claim the next item, carry it to its gate, stop)        | **A — bare `/flow` → Continue the queue**    |
| Make background progress while your terminal is closed, and you run DorkOS | **B — the Pulse seat** (enable `flow-drain`, then approve it) |
| Drive the loop on a non-DorkOS repo with no server                         | **C — the watcher** (when it ships)          |

A short way to decide: **attended and server-free → A. Unattended and you run DorkOS → B.
Portable, no server, non-DorkOS repo → C, once it lands.** A and B carry work to the _same_ review
gate and stop there; the only difference is who keeps the loop ticking.

## How much it does on its own

In DorkOS, each project has one dial in its Flow settings: **Ask me first**, **Tell me after** or
**Just do it**. It decides who answers each kind of ask, and whatever you pick, someone checks.

- **Ask me first.** Flow asks you in the Activity inbox before it ships finished work, and an
  agent's question waits for you. New ideas wait until you press **Sort them**, and failing checks
  until you press **Fix it**.
- **Tell me after.** The reviewer agent ships work it reviewed clean. An agent's question shows its
  own pick and a deadline, and if you haven't answered by then, the agent goes with its pick. Flow
  sorts new ideas each morning and fixes failing checks by itself. Each of these leaves an unread
  note in Activity.
- **Just do it.** The same, with quiet notes, and agents go with their own pick without asking,
  writing down why.

**Where it starts:** a new project starts at **Tell me after**, so its reviewer agent can ship
without asking you. A project flow already worked in stays at **Ask me first** until you choose,
and so does one run without DorkOS.

A question about secrets or spending always waits for you, and nothing on the calibration floor is
settled by a deadline alone. **Customize** sets one kind apart from the dial. The full table is in
[The dials](/docs/guides/flow/the-dials#how-much-it-does-on-its-own).

**While you were away.** What flow settled without asking shows in Activity as one row saying who
decided, such as "Ship the new sidebar? · Shipped · the reviewer agent at 2:14pm". Several in a
row fold into one "While you were away" group. None of them sends a notification.

## Staying in control

Autonomy is never a loss of control, in any setup:

- **`/flow:status`** renders one pane from the tracker plus `flow-state.json`: every claimed and
  in-flight item (worktree, branch, session), every parked `needs-input` question, and the
  assumption trail behind each autonomous decision.
- **`/flow pause`** halts every mode from one place; **`/flow resume`** lifts it. It pauses the
  terminal drain and writes a pause flag into your project (`.agents/flow/paused.json`) that every
  scheduled tick checks first, so the tick stops at once under any scheduler and a plugin update
  cannot undo the pause. On DorkOS it also switches this project's flow schedules off on the
  Schedules page when it can reach them, and resume switches back on only those. Elsewhere the
  scheduler still starts each tick on time and the tick stops at its first step; disable your cron
  or CI job to stop it starting at all.
- **A pause can end on its own.** In DorkOS every Pause asks how long: until tomorrow 9am, for 1
  hour, or until you resume. The Flow tab then says "Paused until 9:00" with Resume beside it, and
  the command palette can pause or resume one project or all of them. In a chat, `/flow pause for 1
  hour` or `/flow pause until 9am` does the same. The end is written into the flag and flow reads it
  itself, so the pause ends on time even with DorkOS closed. A timed pause leaves your schedules on:
  each tick stops at its first step until the end. Resume lifts it sooner.
- **Reprioritize or disable a loop** by editing its `loops.<id>` block.
- **Reclaim or redirect an item** by reassigning it; the running tick honors the change at the next
  stage boundary.

## Still the server build

To keep this honest, here is what "autonomy" does **not** yet include. These pieces are deferred to
the P5 DorkOS server build; the v1 harness is typed and prose-driven, and the always-on autonomous
runner is the server.

<Callout type="warn">

Deferred to the P5 server build:

- **The always-on running scheduler** that executes the reconciler registry continuously and
  unattended. v1 invokes the reconciler model from prose, per tick.
- **Approval detection outside DorkOS.** In DorkOS, approving from the inbox (or the reviewer
  agent's approval) arms the approved commit when `gates.review.mergeOnApproval` is on. Without
  DorkOS you merge the approved PR and run `/flow:done`.
- **Atomic concurrency and fencing** (heartbeats, atomic multi-claim, a stall detector). v1 stays
  `sequential`, WIP-1.
- **The webhook transport.** v1 ships `PollingTransport` and the seam; the webhook producer is the
  deferred drop-in (it needs an inbound endpoint we do not have).
- **Linear Agent Accounts** as the two-account identity backend.

</Callout>

What _is_ real: readiness produced at TRIAGE and DECOMPOSE; starvation surfaced; the reconciler
registry and `loops` config; the `FlowRun` store and recovery; polling for tracker events;
question routing with resume; `/flow:status`, timed and open-ended pauses, `/flow resume`; and the
calibration floor, always checked.
